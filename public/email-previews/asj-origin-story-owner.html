Upala, the first recordings, and a program I almost didn’t believe in.

Before any of this, there was Upala

Hi Maria,

This program will always have a special place in my heart.

So let me tell you how it began.

Upala — my late wife — was recovering from the surgery that saved her life after her brain tumour. The doctors had given her one year. She didn't want to spend it resting. She wanted to travel, and to share the power of authentic singing with the world.

We had no money left in the bank. And I had never recorded anything like this before.

This was before Covid. There weren't many online programs combining music and guided sessions back then. It was new — to me, and to the first people who signed up.

I remember the self-doubt. Will this work? Will people believe me when I say — recorded, at the end of the first session — much love to you? Will the music be good enough? Will anyone actually pay for this?

For those first recordings, Upala almost literally held my hand while I spoke into the mic and played.

Upala beside me.

And then we did start travelling. And people did sign up. And they loved it.

First came the Dutch program — now available again on the site — and later the English one. Our community grew from tens of people to thousands, all over the world, discovering their own authentic voices.

As the months passed, Upala grew stronger, lighter, full of purpose. So we kept going further. A community member in South Africa reached out and invited us to hold retreats there. So we went.

She mostly just taught by being. By living.

Then Corona happened. By then the Authentic Singing Journey was finished — forty weeks to build, one session a week, and another forty for the English version. So we found a new way to stay close to everyone: live, fully improvised mantra sessions on YouTube.

And of course, other programs followed. But more on those in the next email.

You already own this program — thank you for that. Today might be a good day to enjoy another session.
