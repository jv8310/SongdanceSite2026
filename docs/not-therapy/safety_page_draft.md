## How we keep this work careful

*What our courses are, what they are not, and what we ask of you.*

### This is education

Our courses teach a method. The practice is somatic sounding; with the framework around it, we
call it Somatic Vocal Healing.

You can only learn it one way: by doing it, with your own voice, on what is true for you that day.
So personal things move while you learn. Feelings surface. Often something eases. Healing happens
here, and we are glad when it does.

It comes along with the learning. It is not what the course is for, and it is not something we do
to you.

### This is not therapy

Our courses are not therapy and not treatment. They do not replace either, and they do not replace
medical care. Nobody here diagnoses, treats or prescribes.

> "It will always remain valuable to have regular check-ups with someone who knows you and you
> trust. This is not replacing that."

The facilitators hold space. You make the sound, and what heals, heals in you.

The live sessions (classes, Q&As, deepening sessions) are for learning the method and asking about
it. They are group sessions. They cannot hold one person's process, and nobody receives an
individual session there.

### What each course is for

**The 12-week course** is your own immersion. Twelve weeks to learn these tools well enough to
hold space for yourself.

**The certification course** teaches you to hold space for the voice of another. It is not a
therapist training. If you work with clients, you bring your own ground for holding emotional
process.

**The Grief and Forgiveness courses** teach ways of being with grief, and with forgiveness, in the
body and the voice. They are courses, taught to a group.

**The retreats** go deeper and last days. That is why every guest fills in an intake and speaks
with Jacob before a place is confirmed.

Holding space for yourself is a real skill and it can carry you a long way. It is never a
replacement for therapy or for professional help.

### How the practice itself is built

The care in this work is not a rule we added afterwards. It is in how the practice is done.

- **You set the volume.** "The voice will never force you. It always respects your rhythm and
  tempo." Softer when something is big. A hum counts.
- **You make the sound *of* a feeling; you don't become it.** The body stays soft and the voice
  carries it. You stay the witness.
- **One pace back.** When something is too big to sound directly, you make the sound of the energy
  around it.
- **We acknowledge; we don't push.** Nobody here will ask you to scream, and nobody chases a
  breakthrough.
- **Stopping is always allowed.** So is sitting a round out, and so is silence.

In our experience the voice seldom brings up more than you can be with. Seldom is not never. Deep
material can arrive faster than expected, and no way of working can promise otherwise.

### What we mean by trauma-informed

We use the word for how we work, not as a title.

- Everything is an invitation. You choose whether to sound, whether to share, and how much. **[confirm]**
- We don't ask for your story. The practice works with the sound, not the details. **[confirm]**
- When something big comes up in a group, the facilitator slows down and makes it smaller. They
  don't go deeper in front of the room. **[confirm]**
- Before a retreat, and before the courses that touch the most, we ask you some questions first
  (see below). **[confirm, once the intake exists]**
- Sometimes we say "not now". That is care, not a judgment of you.

### What we ask of you

You stay responsible for your own process and your own wellbeing.

If you live with a complex diagnosis, take medication for your mental health, or know dissociation
from the inside: talk with your therapist or doctor before you begin, and tell us in the intake.
Many people practise alongside their therapy.

Keep the support you have. If the work runs deep, you need good support on an ongoing basis, and a
course is one part of that at most.

If you are in crisis now, a course is not the place to start. Please reach a doctor, a therapist
or a crisis line where you live first. The course will still be here.

### The intake

After you sign up for a course or a retreat, and before it opens, we ask a few questions: what
brings you, whether you have support around you, and whether there is anything we should talk
about first.

Jacob and the facilitators of your course read your answers. Nobody else does. A tool helps them
read carefully; a person decides.

Most people are in straight away. Sometimes Jacob will call you first. Sometimes we will both see
that this is not the moment. **[the refund sentence, once decided]**

You can see, change or delete your answers at any time. More in our privacy policy.

### If something comes up while you practise

Stop, or go softer. A hum, a hand on your chest, your feet on the floor.

Reach the people who support you. That is what they are there for.

You can write to us at support@songdance.co and we will answer. We are not an emergency service
and we do not read messages around the clock. If you or someone else is in danger, call your local
emergency number.

## Notes on the draft

- **Sources.** The two quotations are Jacob's own, from the copy book (chapters 31 and 47). The
  rest paraphrases chapters 31 to 36 and 47, and the brief's position statement.
- **"Healing happens here."** Your message says healing happens naturally through the educational
  process, so the draft says so once, plainly, and puts it after the learning. The brief's draft
  statement avoids the word. Decide which you want in the canonical statement.
- **"Regulated."** Your message speaks of the intrinsic way the method keeps people regulated. The
  draft describes the mechanisms (volume, witness, one pace back) and leaves the claim itself out,
  because a statement that the method keeps you regulated is a health claim and a safety guarantee,
  both of which the copy book rules out, and a page linked from the terms will be read literally.
- **The line about the certification course** ("you bring your own ground for holding emotional
  process") is already in the workshop emails; here it sits where a buyer will read it.
- **Languages.** Dutch and German versions are needed before the page is linked from the Dutch
  and German course pages. They should be written by a person, not translated by machine.
