The birth of somatic sounding.

This is the last chapter of the story I’ve been telling you. If you missed the beginning, it starts here.

After Upala’s death, I grieved. I sounded. I howled.

I used my voice for all of it — the heartache, the wrenching pain, the gratitude for what had been, the love, the sheer devastation. My voice held it all in a safe expression: it let me truly acknowledge what I was feeling, and in that acknowledgment the pain could finally move.

And only then — through this sounding of my own — did I begin to understand what Upala had been showing me all those times when her speech left her, and she would simply sound what she felt. After more than a decade as a singing facilitator, I saw it: there was a way to use the voice in a fundamentally different way. A different modality entirely.

The problem? It had taken me over ten years to see the obvious — and the people I tried to explain it to were often just as bewildered as I had been. Our society looks for healing from the outside in: we receive sound healing, we chant a mantra and feel better. And yes — both work wonders. And still, there is something else. Something much simpler, much closer to our human evolution:

Making the sound of the body.

The body holds so much, and every day we live the effects of everything it has stored. That, in itself, is perfect — unless somewhere in all that stored energy there is unattended pain, buried grief, withheld anger, amplified fear. Then we’re no longer living from what the present moment offers. We’re living from a body that is reacting from the past.

Good luck being in the now if your body is stuck in the past. You can’t think your way out of it — but you can sound your way toward it.

That is what somatic sounding is: making the sound of what’s stored in the body. Sounded, the stuck energy is finally met — and life can be lived from a new whole, restructured by your own acknowledgment.

It’s a more precise way of describing what healing work actually is: making a new wholeness, by integrating the parts of yourself that had been cast into the shadow — still steering your daily actions, feelings and thoughts from there. Bringing them into the light doesn’t rid you of the pain. It acknowledges it — and so the pain becomes a small part of a new, bigger whole, and instantly carries less weight.

Even the ego belongs in this story — the ego is nothing more than consolidated bodily memory in action. You don’t transcend it; you help it grow up, by integrating what was into a new whole. The bigger it grows, the better — until one day it’s big enough to realize, from lived, embodied experience, that it is the whole universe. And then, only then, it transcends itself. I’m drifting far now — but that’s exactly the point. Somatic Vocal Healing touches all of these layers. It isn’t only about healing. It’s about growing up into a more conscious human being.
