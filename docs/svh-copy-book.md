# PART ONE — THE VOICE WE WERE BORN WITH

---

## 1 · You were born making sound

*Other headlines:* The first thing you ever did was use your voice · Before words, there was this · You arrived sounding · Born loud, made quiet · The first language · Older than your name

> "We were born with a voice. The first thing we do when we come out of the womb is open our mouth — and if all goes well, we cry. We say: *I'm here.*"

Think about that for a second. Before you had a name, before you had a single thought, you had a sound. It announced you. It was accurate.

A baby doesn't manage its feelings. It sounds them. Joy gets a laugh. Pain gets the sound of pain. Fright, hunger, delight — each one voiced the moment it arrives, and then it's gone, and the baby gets on with the serious business of being a baby. No backlog. No story. No meeting about it afterwards.

> "In the first months and years of our life we are very true to our instinct. We voice how we feel. If we're joyful, we laugh. If we're in pain, we make the sound of pain. That's the essence of Somatic Vocal Healing — helping adults tap back into that innate ability."

That ability never left you. It just went quiet.

> "I don't have to teach you how to do it. I just have to bring your awareness back to what you've never forgotten — that you still know how."

---

## 2 · We lost the voice

*Other headlines:* Somewhere along the way, we went quiet · The long road from instinct · A very old forgetting · The keys are in the house · How a voice gets misplaced

Nobody decided this. It happened the way most losses happen — politely, gradually, with good reasons at every step. *Not so loud. Not here. Not now. Use your words.* And we did. We used our words, and only our words, for everything.

Maybe it wasn't safe to use your voice as a child. Maybe you brought an uncomfortable truth, and you learned to keep it inside. Maybe nothing dramatic happened at all — you just grew up somewhere where feelings were discussed, briefly, and then filed.

> "I've never had a therapist ask me, *could you make a sound for that?* It's odd and sad that it doesn't happen — because it's still so natural to us."

The voice didn't break. It's not gone. It's just out of reach.

> "It's like you have a car and a garage, but you've lost the keys somewhere in the house. The car is right there. You just can't reach it yet."

---

## 3 · Why are our funerals silent?

*Other headlines:* The collective silence · We are ashamed of our sounds · What the old traditions knew · The quietest culture in history · Keening, and what we replaced it with

This isn't only a personal forgetting. It's a cultural one.

> "Why are we silent at a funeral? Funerals are not meant to be silent occasions. There are so many traditions proving the difference — the keening in Ireland, traditions in Eastern Europe, shamanic traditions — where sounding our grief out is common, and powerful, and deeply human. And yet in the West, we are silent."

> "We are so ashamed, collectively, of our sounds."

We built a civilisation with a noise policy. The gym can be loud, the stadium can be loud, the traffic can be loud — but the human being, at the most human moments of a life, is expected to hold it in and say a few appropriate words.

> "Our funerals in the West are silent. We've travelled a long way from our instinct — from being a human who makes sound before they speak."

The work, then, isn't learning something new. It's repealing a very old rule.

*You were loud once. It's still in there.*

---

## 4 · The body is communicating

*Other headlines:* Nothing is wrong with you. Something is talking · The body keeps excellent records · A signal, not a malfunction · Where am I in the process?

> "The body is never wrong. It's just communicating. The traditional view says something is broken and needs fixing. This is the opposite: where am I in the process? What is this telling me?"

That single reframe changes everything about how you meet a tight throat, a heavy chest, a knot in the stomach. Not *what's wrong with me* — but *what is this saying?* The body has been writing to you for years. Sounding is how you finally write back.

> "As long as you breathe, there is communication."

> "Your voice is an ally of the nervous system. If you don't use your voice, the nervous system can stay dysregulated."

*Feelings don't want fixing. They want hearing.*

# PART TWO — THE PRACTICE

---

## 5 · Not singing. Sounding.

*Other headlines:* One tone is the whole instruction · This is not a music lesson · No talent required · The honest instrument · Come as you sound

Sounding is not singing. Not chanting, not performing, not warming up for anything. It is one tone — the tone of what's there, right now. That's the whole instruction.

We say *tone* or *sound*, never *note*. A note belongs to music. A tone belongs to you.

*You don't need a good voice. You need an honest one.*

> "You cannot do it wrong. It will always express something."

If anything, trained singers sometimes have the harder start — the technique stands politely in front of the truth. Years of choirs teach you to make a tone into something. Here, the practice is to make a tone and let it stay nothing but true.

> "Singing is domestication. Sounding is rewilding — it comes back to the body and to nature, rather than the head."

And about the sound itself: stop hoping it will be pretty.

> "When you finally expect an ugly sound — it's the most beautiful sound."

---

## 6 · A small dictionary of the voice

*Other headlines:* Toning, chanting, singing, sounding · Know your instruments · Four doors, one mouth · What each practice actually does

The voice can do several beautiful things, and they are not the same thing.

**Singing** turns feeling into form. Melody, lyrics, shape. Wonderful — and a performance, however private.

**Chanting and mantra singing** connect you with a chosen quality. Lovely words, repeated, often on lovely music.

> "It connects us with whatever these words are — if it's bliss, if it's love. That is very powerful, especially combined with music. But very different from Somatic Vocal Healing."

**Toning** holds a steady tone, often for the body, often soothing.

**Sounding** does something none of the others do: it asks first. It starts from a question — *what is here?* — and lets that answer out, unedited.

> "Somatic Vocal Healing goes in and out at the same time. What is here — and you express it."

One isn't superior to the others. But only one of them tells you the truth about today.

---

## 7 · The sound *of*, not *for*

*Other headlines:* Two small words, the whole method · Nobody plays you here · You are not the audience · The opposite of a sound bath · Of, not for

This is the distinction the whole practice stands on, so let's be precise.

> "We always make the sound *of* something — to give voice to a feeling, a body part, an energy. Traditional sound healing makes sound *for* you: someone plays the bowls, the tuning forks, the voice, and you receive it. That's the older paradigm — something external comes to you and changes you."

In a sound bath, you lie down and receive. Lovely. A different thing. Here, you are the one sounding — nobody does it to you, because nobody can.

> "With Somatic Vocal Healing we start from a different principle: the healing is in the acknowledgment. In being present with. If we can be present with the core of a thing, the thing tends to surrender its need to exist."

There's a friendship version of this that makes it instantly clear:

> "You meet a friend and the friend has an issue. There are two options. You can ask: *what happened? how are you?* Or you can say: *let me give you a cup of tea, here is a blanket.* Two directions. One brings out what is there. The other gives something. One is not superior to the other — they bring different things."

The sound *of* is the question. The sound *for* is the tea. This practice is built on the question.

*Make the sound of it. Not about it.*

---

## 8 · Acknowledgment, not letting go

*Other headlines:* My word for letting go · Why the fire ritual doesn't work · Let it in first · The knot unties itself · Nothing leaves unheard

There are rituals where you write the thing on a little paper and throw it in the fire, to let it go. In my experience, that doesn't really work.

> "My word for letting go is acknowledgment. Everything wants to be acknowledged before it can go away by itself."

> "You cannot let go of a conditioning. You are the one who needed it in the first place. Without understanding why it was needed, you're going to let it go? It's almost like bringing a kid into this world and then saying: I'm going to let go of the kid."

So we go the other way. Not letting go — letting in.

> "The healing path with pain is not letting go — it is letting in. Give it all the space it needs. When pain has been fully acknowledged, it loses its reason to exist and releases itself."

And acknowledgment, here, is not a mental act. It's not saying *I see you* to a feeling and checking a box. It happens in the moment of expression.

> "When I could finally give voice to that pain, I felt the weight come off my shoulders. That's not a mental thing — that's a knot untying."

*Acknowledged things leave on their own.*

---

## 9 · The sound of the moment

*Other headlines:* The core practice · One breath, one tone · How it actually is · The daily question · Thirty honest seconds

The core practice has a name: the sound of the moment. One breath in. One tone out. The tone of how it actually is, right now — not how it should be, not how you'd describe it at dinner.

It takes thirty seconds. It can't be done wrong. And it is always, quietly, the truth.

> "What is the authentic sound you wish to make right now?"

That's the question. Everything else in this work is a variation on it. You can ask it in the morning, in the car, before a difficult conversation, after one. The sound answers before the mind has finished composing its statement.

> "You'll be surprised how much information you can get from your voice. The feelings, the emotions — that's the first thing. That's quite easy. But then there's much more."

And here's what happens underneath: after a couple of minutes of sounding, the brain quiets. The busy daytime frequencies soften toward slower ones, and you become receptive to what usually stays below the surface. The voice is a door to the unconscious that doesn't require a couch.

*A sound cannot procrastinate.*

---

## 10 · One tone tells the truth

*Other headlines:* You can't lie in a tone · Words go single file · The whole of you, at once · Sound before story · The lie detector you carry

> "The voice never lies. Words can lie — the voice never lies."

Words are linear. One after another, single file, choosing as they go. To convey a mixed feeling in words takes ten minutes of context. A tone doesn't have that problem.

> "With sound, we never exit the wholeness of our feelings. We convey them in one sound. We don't have to split them up into segments. That's the beauty."

And you don't need the story first. The sound opens the topic by itself — it brings the image, the memory, sometimes the tears, without the narrative mind getting a vote. You can spend an hour explaining a heartache, or you can make its sound and be standing inside it in four seconds.

One participant put it best:

> "Since learning this, I catch myself the moment I'm not being fully honest — I hear it in my own voice. We're all becoming human lie detectors."

*Talking circles it. Sounding enters it.*

---

## 11 · What is below that?

*Other headlines:* The only question this work really has · One level deeper is enough · Layers, not leaps · Going below · The journey downward

After the first tone, the method is almost embarrassingly simple. You ask: *what is below that?* And you make the sound of that. And then: *what is below that?*

> "You only ever need to go one level deeper for a shift to happen."

Not ten levels. Not the bottom. One. The depth comes from repetition, not force.

Picture a dense forest. There's the anger tree, the grief tree, the mist that hides the path. All of it real, all of it welcome — the trees are there for a reason.

> "But if you keep asking — *what is behind that? what is behind that?* — you're going to land with essence. And then, because you touched the essence, more trees will come. That's all right. We cannot always connect with essence."

And when a connection reveals itself — when you suddenly see what the sound was about — that discovery is yours. Nobody hands it to you.

> "When they discover a connection, leave that gift to them. Don't be the smart one who points it out. This is golden."

*Below every wound, another door.*

---

## 12 · The questions

*Other headlines:* How to ask your voice anything · The craft of the question · Ask, then sound · A question your mind can't answer first

The practice runs on questions, and the questions have a shape: *What is the sound of ___?* You can put nearly anything in the blank. A feeling. A body part. A memory. A relationship. A morning.

The skill is in asking something your mind can't answer faster than your voice can. Ask *am I sad?* and the mind jumps in with an opinion. Ask *what is the sound of this moment?* and the mind has to wait its turn.

> "What is the sound of what's real? I left it to you to fill in — hopefully not with your mind, but with your sound."

One caution from the craft: don't ask the broadest question you can think of. The pain of an entire childhood is a web that runs through work, love, self-care, recurring thoughts — one sound won't clear a web. Ask smaller. Ask about *this* moment, *this* knot, *this* morning's heaviness. The big things are reached the way everything is reached here: one level at a time.

(A full bank of tested questions sits at the back of this book.)

## 13 · There is a map

*Other headlines:* Reading the sound · What the ear learns · Fear is a short wave · Your voice, annotated · Patterns you can hear

A sound carries information, and the information has patterns. High or low. Forward or back. Sharp or round. Steady or trembling. Once you know what to listen for, your own voice becomes readable.

> "If it's a short wave, it's fear. If it's a longer wave, it's grief. And if it's an aesthetic wave — it's a pop singer."

A metallic edge points to anger or frustration. Pain is sharp but contained — it stays where it is, because it refers to a very specific moment. Grief is round and wavering. A creak at the bottom of the voice — the vocal fry — means something is held in the body itself and wants to be trembled loose. The high, small sounds usually belong to the child you were.

> "There's some fear there, there's some grief there, there's some pain that is not mine and needs to go back to my ancestors. All of that is in your sound — quite easy to read once you know the patterns to look out for."

And how do you receive all this information? Not by grabbing at it.

> "When we try too hard, we engage the analytical mind. Receiving is a different orientation — gentle, open, allowing. Passive in effort, active in attention."

One honest note about hearing: we're better trained to hear what's missing than what's present. A whole voice doesn't announce itself. A lacking one does. That, too, is information.

## 14 · What the body does while you sound

*Other headlines:* Yawning is a good sign · Trembling, tears, heat · The body's punctuation · Signals of a system changing state · Read the side effects kindly

Sounding is body work, and the body comments as you go. Learn its punctuation.

**Yawning** — the best one. Not boredom: the nervous system changing state, coming back from far away.

> "You can't go to sleep dissociated. You have to somehow get back first. The yawning is a reflex to really get back."

**Trembling** — something held is loosening. Let it shake.

**Tears** — welcome. Often a part that was never allowed to cry goes first, and the tone follows.

**Heat** — energy that was stored, moving again. Sometimes from very early, wordless places.

**The deep breath that takes itself** — the system's signature at the bottom of a wave. In through the nose, out through the mouth. You'll notice we end everything that way.

None of these are problems. They're receipts.

*The body keeps excellent records — and it signs them.*

---

## 15 · After the sound: the silence

*Other headlines:* The second half of the practice · Listen back · What the echo knows · Don't rush the quiet

Half the practice is the sound. The other half is the three seconds after it.

That's where the information lands. Was it higher than expected? Shakier? Did it surprise you? The sound that surprises you is the one worth following — what you expected to hear, you already knew.

> "There's nothing I did. It was already there."

Don't demand to understand it immediately, either. Give the sound space to be, to exist in itself. Be curious about it rather than interrogating it. Some sounds explain themselves in a minute. Some take a week. Both are fine.

*Expect nothing. Notice everything.*

# PART THREE — THE FEELINGS

---

## 16 · A word about feelings before we start

*Other headlines:* The weather, not the climate · Nothing here is an enemy · Every feeling has a job · Emotion is information in motion

Everything that follows assumes one thing: no feeling in this book is an enemy. Each one exists for a reason, has a job, and shows up in the voice with its own signature. The work is never to defeat a feeling. It's to hear it well enough that it can finish.

> "Everything exists for a reason. Know the reason, understand it, respect it, bow to it."

And one piece of honesty before the tour: this practice does not always feel good first.

> "You might actually first encounter other feelings. You might first feel worse rather than better. *What is this for a stupid modality? I was feeling fine and now I'm all in grief.* Yes — it is a stupid modality if you only want peace. But if you are willing to meet the parts that have been ignored for too long, what's on the other end is a different flavor of peace. More lasting. More structurally embedded in your system."

*Quiet is not the same as peace.*

---

## 17 · Fear — the short wave

*Other headlines:* Fear, audibly · The fast tremble · What fear sounds like · Befriending the alarm

Fear in the voice is a short, fast wave — a quick tremble, like a small animal's heartbeat. Once you've heard it in yourself, you can never quite un-hear it, which turns out to be useful: you learn that you're afraid *before* you've decided what to think about it.

Fear is an alarm, and alarms are loyal. They go off for old reasons long after the danger has moved out. The sound of the fear doesn't argue with the alarm. It acknowledges it — *yes, you're ringing, I hear you* — and rung-and-heard alarms wind down on their own.

When fear tips toward panic, there's a specific tool: make the sound continuous.

> "Can you feel how the continuous sound breaks the fear, breaks the panic, and anchors you again, here and now, in reality? There will still be grief in it — but the continuous sound helps you anchor in the moment."

*An alarm that gets heard can stop ringing.*

---

## 18 · Anger — the metallic edge

*Other headlines:* Anger, without the theatre · Why we don't scream · The forward sound · Heat with a direction

Anger in the voice is metallic, nasal, and it moves forward — *ah!* — out of the body, toward something. Useful information: anger always has an address.

And no, we don't scream it.

> "If you want to work with the wound of anger, screaming is not the way. You make the sound of the anger — the tone of it."

Screaming performs the anger. The tone *acknowledges* it — and those are different acts with different results. (One honest exception: someone who was never allowed to be angry at all may first need permission to be loud. That's its own work. It comes before this one.)

A healthy relationship with anger doesn't sound like rage. It sounds like a voice with that quality available — present, not pushed away, not leaking. You hear health in a voice less by what's there and more by what isn't missing.

*Anger is heat with a direction. Find the address before you send it.*

---

## 19 · Grief — the river

*Other headlines:* Grief is the river, not the knot · When grief comes, it is welcome · The most useful feeling we avoid · Round and wavering · Let the wave through

We treat grief like a problem. It isn't one.

> "Grief is not the pain. It is the process that helps you move through it. Every grief has a core of pain — but grief itself is the river, not the knot."

Grief is life-force. It is how a human being crosses from one chapter to the next. Which is why, in this work, grief gets priority. When it arrives, everything else can wait.

> "When grief arises, prioritize it. Give it all the space. It may not come again soon."

And it's bigger than the loss of a loved one.

> "Grief is such a broad spectrum of feeling. Really, for me, it comes down to the very fact of being human."

There's even a posture for it. Grief wants to fold you forward and close you down. Do the opposite:

> "Lift the head, relax the shoulders, sound the grief outward. The wave moves through in one to two minutes."

Waves pass. That's what waves do.

> "Tomorrow I will grieve, look back, and honour what was. And the day after, I will live, and look forward. Life is for the living. And when grief comes, it is welcome."

---

## 20 · Pain — the signal

*Other headlines:* Pain is not a punishment · The crying baby · A key to a door · Why "just let go" fails · Sharp, and centered

> "Pain is not a punishment. It is a signal — like a crying baby forcing the parents to pay attention. It exists to bring your awareness to something that needs to be seen."

That's why pain is uncomfortable on purpose. A signal you could ignore would be a useless signal.

> "Pain always points somewhere. It holds a key to a door. The pain will not go away without the realization it is pointing toward. This is why *just letting go* of pain does not work."

In the voice, pain is sharp but contained — a centered *ah* that stays where it is, because it refers to a very specific moment. Pain is a dot in an experience. Grief flows; pain points.

Animals understand pain better than we do. They can be with it in the most present, undramatic way — not suffering, just feeling. We humans build stories around ours: who did it, why it's unfair, what it means about us. The stories are understandable. They're also extra weight. The voice goes underneath the story — and the signal finally gets received. The baby can stop crying, because the parent finally came.

*Pain is a pointer. Look where it points.*

---

## 21 · Joy — yes, joy too

*Other headlines:* The sound we forget to make · Joy counts as practice · Sparkle, audibly · Not only the heavy things

A confession from inside this work: we don't hear joy often enough here. People bring their voices to the difficult things — rightly — and forget the instrument works in every direction.

> "Thank you. It's nice for a change, right? We don't get to hear these sounds so often in this space."

Joy has a sound — a sparkle, an upward ease — and sounding it does something subtle: it lets the body register that the good moment actually happened. We are quick to metabolise our troubles and strangely careless with our delights. So: the sound of the swim. The sound of the morning that went well. The sound of nothing being wrong, for once.

And sometimes joy surprises you from inside the heavy work, because it was waiting underneath. That, too, is information.

*Your strengths can be a blind spot too. Sound those.*

---

## 22 · The flat ones — when nothing seems to be there

*Other headlines:* The sound of numb · Flat is a feeling too · The dorsal weather · When the voice goes grey

Some days the answer to *what is the sound of the moment?* is grey. Flat. A nothing-sound. People apologise for it, as if numbness disqualified them from the practice.

It doesn't. Flat is a feeling too — the body's dimmer switch, the dorsal weather of a system that learned to power down. It has its own sound: low, toneless, unsparkling. Make it anyway. Numbness, like everything else here, wants to be acknowledged before it will consider moving.

And when even the outer voice won't come, there's a doorway called soundalisation: making the sound internally, silently, fully imagined. The body hears it. It counts.

*Grey is a colour. Sound it.*

---

## 23 · Mixed feelings — the whole forecast at once

*Other headlines:* Emotional multitasking · More than one thing is true · The chord, not the note · You are never one feeling

You are never only one feeling. Happy to be here *and* frustrated from the day. Grieving *and* relieved. Words force you to pick; the voice doesn't.

> "We are never only one emotion. We are always a being. Feelings happen at the same time, and your sound gives expression of that."

A tone is a chord, not a single line. Metallic *and* wavy means anger *and* sadness, both true, both present, neither cancelling the other. This is one of the kindest things the practice teaches: you don't have to resolve yourself into one tidy state before you're allowed to express. The whole weather system is welcome in one breath.

*More than one thing is true. The tone can hold them all.*

---

## 24 · The old wounds

*Other headlines:* The wounds with names · Abandonment, rejection, betrayal, humiliation, injustice · Old rooms, known doors · A short tour of the deep ones

Underneath the daily feelings live the older ones — the wounds with names: abandonment, rejection, betrayal, humiliation, injustice. Teachers have mapped them for decades. The voice lets you visit each one and hear, with unusual precision, which doors are open in you.

Each wound sounds different in each person — sharp for one, hollow for another, a soundless gasp, a low moan, a sound that arrives easily and a sound that won't come at all. All of it is information. The wound that gets five stars in your own listening is the one worth returning to — gently, one level at a time, never all five in an afternoon.

That last part matters: don't process the whole catalogue in one sitting. These are old rooms. You visit; you don't move in.

*Old rooms. Known doors. One visit at a time.*

# PART FOUR — DEEPER WATERS

## 25 · The inner child — the high, small sounds

*Other headlines:* Who makes the high sounds · The child still speaks · Small voice, old truth · Listening down the years

When the sounds come out high and small, you've usually met the child. The inner child doesn't speak in arguments. It speaks in pitch.

And it remembers in the present tense. An old fear can look out through adult eyes at a perfectly safe Tuesday and report danger. The work is not to scold that child for bad reporting. It's to let it speak — in sound — and then gently introduce it to the current facts:

> "These are the eyes of the child looking at my current reality. This is the scared heart of the child beating in my current reality. But I, here and now, know that I am safe. And that I'm not being hurt — but that I'm being heard."

*Being heard* is the operative phrase. It's what the child was waiting for, often for decades. The voice can give it in minutes what explanations couldn't give it in years.

*Small voice. Old truth. Finally heard.*

---

## 26 · The pain that isn't yours

*Other headlines:* Some of what you carry came with the family · Give it back · The line behind you · What the ancestors couldn't finish · Inherited weather

Some of what you carry has your name on it. Some of it doesn't. It came with the family — pain from people who lived before you, who never got the chance, the tools, or the permission to acknowledge it themselves. You weren't there when it happened. And yet here it is, in you.

You can hear it. Pain that isn't yours sounds different — and once it's recognized, something remarkable becomes possible: you can give it back. Not as rejection. As respect.

> "This is the sound of the fear in the system. This fear from the system — I'm giving back. And please support me, so I am able to move forward."

> "It is the brave who receive."

One distinction matters here. Grief flows through a family the way a river flows through land — it can be shared, and often should be. But another person's *pain* cannot be carried productively. Not even out of love. Especially not out of love.

*Give back what was never yours. With a bow, not a shove.*

---

## 27 · Conversations in sound

*Other headlines:* Speaking to the line behind you · A dialogue without arguments · The constellation principle · Any system can be given sound

Once you can sound, you can converse — with a parent, living or gone; with a grandfather you never met; with a chapter of your life; with the family system itself.

> "Any system can be constellated — a family, a working relationship, an inner conflict, an unfinished chapter of a life. We give it sound and let it reorganise."

These conversations skip the part where everyone defends themselves. There are no rebuttals in a tone. You make the sound of what was never said; you let the other position have its sound too; and something in the system, hearing itself accurately for the first time, shifts its weight.

> "In a retreat we have the room, the group, the field. A system can be set up and given sound — and it reorganises in front of you."

You don't need the people present. You don't even need them alive. The system is in you; the sound reaches it there.

*No rebuttals in a tone.*

## 28 · Before words, before memory

*Other headlines:* The wordless years · Pre-verbal, still present · What happened before you could say so · The earliest rooms

The deepest things that shaped you happened before you could narrate them. The first months. The womb. The wordless years when everything was felt and nothing was filed under language.

Talking can't reach what was never in words. The voice can — because the voice was there first.

> "Including early life, pre-verbal and even prenatal experience that still lives in the body — and that your voice can bring up."

No memory is required. No story. You make the sound, and a body that has been holding something since before your first sentence finally gets to say it in its native language: tone.

This is gentle work, and it titrates itself like everything here — the voice only opens what you can be with. But it explains something people often feel in their first sessions: *why does this reach so deep, so fast?* Because it goes in below the floor where words are kept.

*The voice was there before the words. It still knows the way.*

## 29 · The wound under all the wounds

*Other headlines:* Separation · What every hurt has in common · Below the last layer · And underneath: joy · The softest paradox

Keep asking *what is below that?* long enough, and every road arrives at the same place.

> "Everything we experience as something that could harm us can only exist if separation exists. My parents didn't hear me, didn't love me — I was betrayed, abandoned, humiliated. It is always happening from one entity towards another entity. Go below every wound, and below, and below, and you will end up at the wound of separation. And below that: joy, oneness, love."

Now, carefully — because this is exactly where healing talk usually goes wrong:

> "I don't say this to deny or bypass trauma. The pain needs to be felt. The injustice needs to be set right. I'm not saying go there directly. I'm saying: if it is felt, and acknowledged, and you go below — that is what you find."

You can't skip to the love. But it's there.

> "It is true that we are separate, and it is true that we are one, at the same time. Maybe in the softness of that paradox, there's also healing."

## 30 · The transpersonal — rare, and not yours to schedule

*Other headlines:* What can't be forced · Grace has its own calendar · The room you don't unlock yourself · When the bottom opens

Sometimes — rarely — a journey through the layers opens into something that isn't a layer anymore. The love under everything. The joy under the greatest traumas. People come back from those moments changed, and quieter.

You cannot book this. Nobody can take you there, and trying to force it is how good work turns into bypass.

> "It is not for you to decide or force your way here. It comes by itself when the foundation is laid — when enough attention has been given to the pain."

So the practice stays humble. We attend to what's actually here, layer by honest layer. And once in a while, by a grace that keeps its own calendar, the floor opens onto the thing the mystics kept pointing at and could never quite say.

> "The essence is not a frequency. The essence is a song."

*You can't skip to the love. But it's there.*

# PART FIVE — SAFETY, RESISTANCE, AND THE SELF

## 31 · The voice will never force you

*Other headlines:* Safety, built in · The careful gatekeeper · Soft counts double · Your rhythm, your tempo · Volume is a dial, not a dare

Here's what makes this work safe to do: the safety isn't a rule we added. It's built into the instrument.

> "The voice will never force you. It always respects your rhythm and tempo."

The voice is a careful gatekeeper. It only brings up what you can be with. And volume is yours to set — softer when something is big, barely a hum if that's the day. Soft counts. Soft often counts double.

> "Your voice is an ally of the nervous system. If you don't use your voice, the nervous system can stay dysregulated."

This is also why we don't override the voice with willpower — no forcing louder, no pushing past a close. A practice built on overriding teaches the body one more time that its signals don't matter. This practice is built on the opposite lesson.

If you yawn — good. If you tremble — good. If the most you can manage today is a hum into a cushion — that was a complete practice.

*The voice asks permission. Always.*

---

## 32 · One pace back — the sound of the energy

*Other headlines:* Too hot to touch directly · A respectful distance · The step-removed sound · How to approach the big ones

When something feels too big or too hot to sound directly, there is a step-removed version: make the sound of the *energy* of it. Not the abuse — the energy of that time. Not the loss itself — the energy around it. Still true. Still acknowledging. One respectful pace back.

This isn't avoidance. It's titration — the difference between opening a door and taking it off its hinges. From the energy, you can always move closer when the body says yes. And the body will say yes, in its own time, because acknowledged edges soften.

The same principle works for the protective layers themselves. The wall you built around you? It has a sound. The numbness? It has a sound. Even the mechanism that takes you away from yourself:

> "We make the sound of the dissociation — because that's the mechanism that has been trying to help you survive. We're not judging that mechanism. We're honoring it. And by making its sound, we acknowledge it — and it can melt."

*Even your defenses deserve to be heard. Start there if you need to.*

---

## 33 · Put it in the tone

*Other headlines:* The body stays soft · Don't become the feeling — sound it · Witnessing distance · The energy goes to the voice

There's a posture to this work, and it's the opposite of method acting. We don't clench into the feeling, re-enact it, become it. We give it to the tone.

> "Instead of the body taking on that energy, the body can remain soft and relaxed — and the voice will express it."

This is the difference between sounding *while in* an emotion and making the sound *of* it. Inside the storm, you're weather. One step back, voicing the storm, you're the witness — and witnessing is where acknowledgment lives. The bird's eye and the open throat, at the same time.

It looks like a small technical note. It's actually the whole safety architecture: the feeling gets full expression, and you get to stay home in your body while it happens.

*Be the witness with a voice, not the storm with a body.*

## 34 · When the sound won't come

*Other headlines:* Resistance, respectfully · The closed throat · Nothing is broken · Doors that need a moment

Some days you sit down, ask the question, open your mouth — and nothing. Throat closed. Tears instead. Or just a polite internal *no*.

Nothing is broken. Resistance is a guard at a door, and guards exist for reasons. You have options, all of them respectful:

Make the sound *of the resistance itself* — the no has a tone, and it's usually very willing to be heard. Go quieter — a hum counts. Go internal — soundalise it, silently, fully imagined; the body hears it. Or move first: let the body sway, walk, rock, and let sound join the movement later, when it wants to.

And sometimes the honest move is to stop for today. The practice will be there tomorrow. So will you.

> "There's no real right way — for each person, you get there one way or another."

*A closed door has a sound too. Start with that one.*

---

## 35 · The one exception: the sound *for*

*Other headlines:* The tea and the blanket · When we do give · A hand on your own chest · The blanket of sound

Everything in this book is the sound *of*. There is exactly one place where we turn the practice around and make the sound *for* — deliberately, by name.

It's the giving direction. A hand on your own chest, a soothing tone offered *to* that place, the way you'd bring a friend tea and a blanket instead of questions. At gatherings it becomes the sound bath: half the circle lying down, the other half moving around them with gentle sound and gentle touch.

> "There we make the sound *for*, not *of*."

And something honest happens when you try it on yourself: sometimes the place under your hand refuses the gift. The soothing tone cracks into expression — *not yet, I need to say something first.* Let it. Expression first, comfort after. Then the tea gets drunk.

> "You are the emitter and the receiver at the same time. If you put your hand on your cheek — is your cheek feeling your hand, or your hand feeling your cheek?"

*First the saying. Then the soothing.*

## 36 · You are the healer

*Other headlines:* Nobody does this to you · You are the healer, and you are the healed · What a space holder actually does · It was already there · Help that doesn't take over

> "You are the healer, and you are the healed. You don't go anywhere else. You use that which you were born with."

This is not modesty. It's mechanics. Nobody can make your sound for you. Which means nobody can do your acknowledging for you — and acknowledging is where the healing is.

So what does a facilitator do? They hold space. They ask the next honest question. They hear what you can't hear yet. And then they get out of the way.

> "There's nothing I did. It was already there."

> "Healing is a way of more allowing — creating more space for the unexpected to open even new spaces where more allowing is possible."

The word for this role is *space holder*. Not healer, not fixer. Someone who keeps the room steady while you do the one thing only you can do.

*We make room. The rest happens.*

# PART SIX — LIVING IT

## 37 · The neighbours, the shame, the first time

*Other headlines:* About the neighbours · Everyone asks this · Start in the bathroom · The first sound is the longest · One day you stop caring

The most common question in this work is not about trauma or ancestors. It's about the neighbours.

> "Usually what happens is this: for a long time, people don't dare. And then suddenly something happens — and they don't care anymore, and they just make the sound."

Until that day: a cushion. The bathroom. The car — half this practice happens in parked cars. Be patient with yourself and take it in steps. The first sound is the longest. Every one after it is shorter.

And know that this phase is real, not a personal failing:

> "There's a phase you have to go through. I went through it, and it can take years before one gets to the point of: I make my sound, and they think what they think."

Even sensitive people who've practised for years can feel the walls listening. That's not weakness. That's sensitivity, doing its job. Work with it, not against it.

*Start in the bathroom. Everyone does.*

## 38 · Crying

*Other headlines:* Tears first, tone after · The cry that was postponed · Crying is allowed · What the tears begin, the sound carries

If you cry — then you cry. Tears are welcome here. Sometimes a part of you that was never allowed to cry needs exactly that first: the cry that was postponed thirty years, finally happening, with someone (even just you) saying *have at it. I'm here to hold you.*

And then, when the wave allows, you put it in the tone — and the sound carries what the tears began. The tears open the door; the sound walks through it and says what was behind it. They're not competitors. They're a relay.

There's also a third option, useful on days when the tears would swallow everything: the bird's eye. One step back, the sound of the sadness rather than drowning in it. Crying *in* it and sounding *of* it are both honest. You get to choose, moment by moment, which one serves.

*Crying is allowed. So is everything else.*

## 39 · A practice for ordinary days

*Other headlines:* Three breaths and a tone · The sound of the day · Healing between the dishes · A practice that fits in a kitchen · Not an eternal excavation

This is not a retreat-only practice. It's a kitchen practice. A car practice. A practice for the two minutes before the day takes over.

> "I recommend a morning practice — make it part of the routine you already have. Just: *how am I feeling, in this moment?* And make the sound of that. You can do the same in the evening, like a checkout."

Some people set a quiet timer through the day. Every hour: one breath, one tone, back to work. That's the whole apparatus. Others keep it to thresholds — before the difficult call, after it, on the doorstep coming home, so the day's weather doesn't walk into the house with you.

One warning, and it matters: this is a tool, not a destination. Don't go digging for the sake of digging. There is no prize for being eternally in process. You sound, you acknowledge, and then you go live your life — which was the point all along.

> "This is how a tree grows — slow and steady."

*The kitchen is a fine temple.*

## 40 · In the moment it actually happens

*Other headlines:* Healing at the speed of life · Not in three weeks — now · The appointment your feelings won't wait for · First aid for the inside

Here's a quiet problem with how we've organised emotional life: the feeling happens on Tuesday, and the appointment is in three weeks.

> "When I have something I want to work on — a moment of crisis — by the time the appointment comes, three weeks later, it's all gone, done and dusted."

Regular support from someone who knows you remains valuable — this work never replaces that. But there is another need, a different need: something you can do *in the moment*, with no equipment, no booking, no one else. The sound of the moment is exactly that. The feeling arrives; the feeling gets voiced; the feeling gets acknowledged at the moment of its actual occurrence, which is when it's most workable and most honest.

First aid doesn't replace the doctor. But you'd never run a life without it.

*Your feelings don't keep office hours. Neither does your voice.*

## 41 · Sounding together

*Other headlines:* The circle · Witnessed, not watched · Why a group changes everything · You don't have to do it alone · Compassion, out loud

Sounding alone is one thing. Sounding where others can hear you is another. Being in a room together, hearing each other's real voices — that is another thing again.

Something happens in a circle that doesn't happen alone: you witness someone make the authentic sound of their life, and compassion arrives in you without being asked. Not pity. Recognition. And something happens to *your* sound when it's witnessed, too — being heard changes what dares to come out.

> "Community is the antidote to isolation. You don't have to do it alone."

There's even a strange intimacy in the shared field, named best by Jacob in a moment of group grief:

> "Your grief is my grief, your pain is my pain — because we are connected on that level. But your story is not my story. The feelings are collective. The stories are our own."

> "This is not about some far-reaching theory or some concepts. This is about what it is to be human."

*Witnessed is different from watched.*

## 42 · At the thresholds

*Other headlines:* Sound at the beginning, sound at the end · The two doors of a life · What the old midwives knew · Accompaniment, in tone

A human life is bracketed by two thresholds, and both of them were always accompanied by sound — until recently, until us.

A newborn is welcomed by voices. And the dying, in older traditions, were sounded across: keened, sung, hummed to, accompanied to the very edge by the human instrument. We replaced that with hush, as if quiet were the same as respect.

It isn't, always. A voice can hold a hand that hands can no longer hold. Tone reaches where conversation has already said goodbye. People who have sounded at a deathbed — softly, just breath with a little voice in it — describe it the same way: *I didn't know what to say, so I stopped saying, and stayed.*

This is old knowledge wearing new clothes. The practice simply gives you back the instrument for it.

*Where words have finished, the voice can stay.*

## 43 · From sound into song

*Other headlines:* When the tone wants a melody · The song of the moment · Music is allowed too · What returns again and again

Sometimes a sound doesn't want to stay a tone. It starts to move, finds a second pitch, becomes — almost by itself — the song of the moment. Let it, with one honest check: is this the feeling deepening, or the feeling escaping into beauty?

> "Your cup will tell you. You will feel the difference."

Both happen. A melody can be a gift rising out of the truth — or a lovely exit from it. When in doubt, return once to the single tone — *whatever is here, one sound for it* — and see what's actually present. Then sing all you like.

Because music belongs in this life too. Songs around a piano, mantras on good harmonies, the sheer pleasure of a voice doing something beautiful — none of that is the enemy. It's just a different door. This practice exists so that when you sing, you'll be singing *from* yourself, not instead of yourself.

*First the truth. Then the tune.*

## 44 · What the animals know

*Other headlines:* The original practitioners · Nature never went quiet · A dog has no backlog · Rewilding the throat

Watch a dog shake off a fright. Watch a cat stretch and groan. Watch any animal meet pain — fully, presently, without a committee meeting about what it means. Then watch it finish, and move on. No backlog. No story.

Animals never lost the practice, because they never had the language to replace it with. We did — and the trade was real, but it cost us. We gained explanation and lost expression. The work here is not to become less human. It's to be a human with the animal still intact: the one creature that can do both — feel it fully, sound it truly, *and* understand it afterwards.

> "Sounding is rewilding — it comes back to the body and to nature, rather than the head."

*A dog has no backlog. Be a person with one less, daily.*

## 45 · Where this began

*Other headlines:* Upala · Before it had a name · Taught by being · The essence melody · The origin

> "My late wife passed away from a brain tumour. As she lost her speech, she started to sound — before any of this was a method, before we had a name for it. She used it to communicate with me, and to acknowledge her own feelings. And to my surprise, every time she did, she'd move from turmoil — frustration, grief, fear — to something like bliss, in a couple of minutes. Her name was Upala.

> "She mostly just taught by being. By living."

> "She'd go from the sound of the moment into the song of the moment, very naturally, and one melody would return again and again. That was the Upala essence melody. Sometimes it still flows out of my mouth, and it always touches me, because I feel the essence."

> "Her smile and her love and her dedication for this work — for me, she ignited it. It remains a source of power that pushes us forward."

And the man who carries it forward:

> "I'm a self-taught, self-certified man. I came to these teachings through my own life experience — not from books, not from a teacher. So who am I to say you need a certificate to do something?"

## 46 · Why this practice could only exist now

*Other headlines:* New tool, old voice · Why the ancients didn't do this · Standing on two shoulders · A modern practice with ancient material

A fair question: if sounding is so natural, why isn't there a thousand-year-old tradition of exactly this?

Because half of it couldn't exist yet. The old paths — shamanic, contemplative, devotional — were magnificent at the waking-up work: transcendence, oneness, the upward door. But they had no map of the psyche. No concept of the unconscious, of conditioning, of how a childhood shapes an adult. Psychology hadn't been invented.

> "Getting a transcendental experience is not the same as having a clear map of how we develop and what roadblocks are on that line. With each great evolution — the alphabet, the invention of psychology — humanity is able to go into next-level practices."

Somatic Vocal Healing stands on both shoulders: the oldest instrument a human owns, and the newest understanding of what a human is. Ancient material, modern map. That combination is why it can be gentle *and* go deep — and why nobody could have built it sooner.

*The voice is ancient. The map is new. We needed both.*

## 47 · What this is not

*Other headlines:* Honest small print · No miracles, on purpose · What we won't promise you · The disclaimers we mean · Read this part too

This is not therapy, and it doesn't replace it.

> "It will always remain valuable to have regular check-ups with someone who knows you and you trust. This is not replacing that."

And when the healing work runs deep, hear this clearly:

> "Somatic Vocal Healing is an aspect of the journey. When we have such deep healing work to do, it goes into all aspects of life — and you need good support on an ongoing basis."

It's not a singing course. It's not a sound bath. It's not screaming into the void — we don't go to screaming. It's not a shortcut around your pain; it's the dignified road through it. And it doesn't always feel good first — see chapter 16; we mean that.

About the body, only this much, and honestly: some people arrive here because of something physical the doctors couldn't reach — and even if it isn't healed, changing your relationship to it can make a real difference. That's the most we'll claim, because it's the most we know.

> "Don't expect miracles — expect a real relationship with your own voice."

We'd rather under-promise. The work doesn't need exaggerating.

## 48 · The sound was always yours

*Other headlines:* Begin below the words · One tone, today · The keys were in the house · Closing the book, opening the mouth

The car is in the garage. The keys are in the house. They were never lost — just set down somewhere, years ago, by a child who was told *not so loud*.

So: one tone. Today, in the kitchen, soft as you like. The sound of how it actually is. Then the breath — in through the nose, out through the mouth — and back to your life, which is where all of this was pointing the whole time.

> "Life is meant to be felt."

*The sound was always yours.*
